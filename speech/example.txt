1. Demo
Good morning, everyone.
Today I would like to demo a simple AI safety feature for AMR.
When the AMR detects a person nearby, it immediately stops.
Even when it's moving, it immediately stops.
Even when it's carrying materials, it immediately stops.
It looks simple, but in a human-robot environment, safety always comes first.
 


2. Vision 
However, this project didn't start with human detection.
It started with a problem that LiDAR couldn't solve.
LiDAR is very good at measuring distance and detecting obstacles.
But it doesn't know what the obstacle is.
For example, it cannot tell the difference between a glass floor and an open maintenance hole.
That's why we introduced AI Vision on the AMD AI MAX's NPU.
Now the AMR can understand what an obstacle is, not just detect it.



3. Value
Let's take a familiar example.
Automatic doors in a supermarket don't usually need AI.
An infrared sensor is enough to detect someone and open the door.
Of course, an AI camera could do the same thing.
But if AI is only used to open the door, we are just using a different technology to get the same result.
AI brings real value by adding new capabilities, not by replacing the infrared sensor.
With AI, the same system can recognize employees for attendance,
analyze customer interest based on how long they stay in front of a product,
or detect restricted areas and issue safety alerts.
AI is not here to replace sensors. It's here to make systems more valuable.



4. Decision 
Once the robot knows what the obstacle is,
it does not have to do the same thing every time.
Now it can choose the best action.
For example:
If it sees a person, it stops and waits.
If it sees a static obstacle, it finds another path.
If it sees a glass floor or an open maintenance hole, it avoids the danger.
It can even recognize a maintenance engineer and follow that person.
The goal is no longer just obstacle avoidance.
It is making the right decision.



5. Reasoning
When AI can do more than see, it begins to understand what is happening and make better decisions.
AMD AI Max+ 395, with up to 128GB of unified memory, allows large language models to run directly on the edge.
Now the robot doesn't just know what it sees.
It understands what is happening and decides what to do next.
Previously, AI followed predefined rules for different situations.
Today, we simply tell the AI our goal.
For example:"Go to Warehouse 2 and bring me the part."
From there, the AI chooses the best way based on the current situation.
If it sees someone, it waits until the person passes.
If people are doing maintenance, it stays away.
If equipment is blocking the path, it finds another way.
We don't need to tell it every step. We only need to tell it the goal.
This is no longer just object detection. It's understanding the situation and completing the task we've given it. 



6. Agent
In fact, AI agents are already becoming part of our daily lives.
When you leave Moscone Center later today, you'll probably see a Waymo self-driving car.
It's like your AI driver. You don't tell it when to turn left or right.
You simply tell it where you want to go.
From there, it watches the road, understands traffic signals, avoids people and cars, and gets you there safely.
This is how an AI agent works. It sees the world, understands it, plans, and completes a task.
If an AI agent can drive on the busy streets of San Francisco,
think about what it can do in a controlled semiconductor environment.
